Case Study — 01 ARX A POS platform for the auto repair industry, designed from scratch to replace manual scheduling, billing and inventory tracking with one clear system.
Role Product Designer
Scope Research · information architecture · UI · usability testing
Method Double Diamond
01 — Problem Statement Small repair shops still run on paper, memory, and WhatsApp updates. As jobs pile up, parts go missing, invoices get delayed, and no one — manager, mechanic, or customer — has one place to check what's actually happening.
— The manager wanted less chaos. — The mechanic wanted fewer back-to-back jobs. — The customer wanted their car yesterday.
Research revealed that many small shop owners still run on manual or semi-digital systems, leading to inventory mismatches, billing delays and operational inefficiencies during peak hours.
02 — Accessibility & Constraints Designed for greasy hands and bad lighting, not a clean office. Real shop-floor conditions shaped every constraint on the interface.
Legibility
Large type and high-contrast text for staff reading screens at arm's length, often without their glasses on.
Touch targets
Buttons sized for gloved or dirty fingers on a shared tablet, not a mouse pointer.
Connectivity
Workshops with patchy Wi-Fi meant every action needed to feel instant, with changes syncing quietly in the background.
03 — Research: Competitive Analysis Existing options were either too generic, or no system at all.

05 — Wireframes Structure first, style later. Every screen started in low-fidelity grayscale so layout and hierarchy could be argued over before a single color was picked.
06 — Results 8 participants. 6 core tasks. One question: does this actually help? Moderated usability sessions with 5 workshop owners/managers and 3 mechanics creating a repair job, assigning a mechanic, tracking status, generating an invoice, checking inventory, and sending updates.
